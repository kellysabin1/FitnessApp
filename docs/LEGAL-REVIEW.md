# Privacy & legal compliance review — rep. (November 24, 2026)

**Read this first:** this is a structured compliance review written by an AI assistant, not legal advice. It
doesn't create an attorney–client relationship and isn't privileged. Its purpose is to get the documents and
the app as close to compliant as possible, so that a licensed attorney's review is short. Laws change: the
"as of" date matters.

**Scope:** the in-app Privacy Policy, Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy and Terms (`src/legal.ts`), the data
inventory (`docs/PRIVACY.md`), and how the app and server actually behave (consent gates, sharing,
deletion, retention, security). Assumes rep. is free, run by one individual in the US, used by a small
number of US users (friends and family now, more later), and doesn't sell data or show ads.

## 1. Which laws apply

| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

Workouts, body measurements, steps, sleep/HR are "consumer health data". Has a private right of action, so it's the highest-risk law here.                                    |
| **Nevada consumer health data law** (SB 370)                                           | Yes, for Nevada users                 | Similar to Washington's.                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Connecticut** consumer health data provisions (CTDPA as amended by SB 3)             | Yes, for Connecticut users            | Health-data consent and no-sale rules apply regardless of size.                                                                                                                                             |
| **FTC Act §5** (unfair/deceptive practices)                                            | Yes                                   | The policy must match what the app actually does.                                                                                                                                                           |
| **FTC Health Breach Notification Rule** (16 CFR 318, amended 2024)                     | **Likely yes**                        | An app that holds health data from more than one source (your typing + Apple Health/Oura) can be a "personal health record" vendor. Breach notice duties, and unauthorized disclosure counts as a breach.   |
| State data-breach laws (all 50 states)                                                 | Yes                                   | Notice duties if personal data is breached.                                                                                                                                                                 |

| **Apple App Store Guidelines 5.1.1 / 5.1.3**                                           | When the iPhone app ships             | Privacy policy link, in-app account deletion, health data not used for ads or sold, consent before sharing with third parties. ## 3. Decisions (resolved 2026-11-24)

- **A. Anthropic retention:** per Anthropic's API data-retention documentation, conversation content isn't
  retained by default for the model rep. uses; flagged content may be kept up to 2 years. Now stated in the
  policy. Re-check if the coach model changes (some model families require 30-day retention). Note for the
  attorney: the server enables Anthropic's server-side model fallback on refusals — confirm the fallback
  model's retention terms match.
- **B. Operator:** Kelly O'Reilly (individual). Consider an LLC before a public launch.
- **C. Privacy contact:** the owner's email, as chosen by the owner. A dedicated address is still worth considering later.
- **D. Governing law:** Virginia. Virginia's own privacy law (VCDPA) doesn't apply yet (100,000-consumer threshold).

## 4. Still needs a licensed attorney before a public launch

- Final sign-off on all three documents, especially Washington MHMDA wording (private right of action).
- Whether rep. is a "personal health record" vendor under the FTC rule, and breach procedures.
- Entity choice (LLC) and whether the $50 liability cap and governing-law clause are enforceable where your users are.
- If you add paid plans, ads, analytics, or sell to employers/gyms: re-review. Several "doesn't apply yet" laws would then apply.

A focused review of documents this complete typically takes a privacy attorney a couple of hours.
